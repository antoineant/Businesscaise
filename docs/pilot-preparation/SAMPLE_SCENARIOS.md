## Scenario 1: Early-Stage Startup

### Company Background

**Company:** CloudFlow Technologies
**Product:** Project management SaaS for remote teams
**Industry:** Tech SaaS
**Stage:** Early-stage startup (18 months old)
**Team Size:** 12 employees
**Funding:** $500K seed round (8 months runway)

**Starting Metrics:**
- Financial: 35/100 (limited revenue, burning cash)
- Operations: 40/100 (MVP working, some bugs)
- Marketing: 30/100 (organic only, no brand)
- HR: 50/100 (passionate but stressed team)
- Customer Satisfaction: 45/100 (early adopters love it, others frustrated)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Growth vs. sustainability trade-offs
- Product-market fit validation
- Resource allocation under constraints
- Fundraising decisions
- Building MVP to scale

### Session Challenges

**SESSION 1: Customer Churn Crisis (Monday AM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Urgent: Customer Churn Spike

You wake up Monday morning to an alarming email from your customer success manager: 3 customers canceled their subscriptions over the weekend. That's 15% of your paying customer base gone in 48 hours.

Exit interview feedback:
• "Too many bugs - lost work twice"
• "Features promised 3 months ago still not delivered"
• "Customer support takes 2+ days to respond"

Your Current Situation:
• 17 paying customers remaining ($49/month each = $833 MRR)
• Engineering team focused on building new features
• 1 person doing support part-time (not enough)
• Cash runway: 8 months

You have three options to consider:

A. STABILIZE: Stop all feature development. Fix bugs and improve reliability.
B. DOUBLE-DOWN: Accelerate feature development to keep promises. Hire support person.
C. PIVOT: Listen to churned customers, rebuild product around their needs.
D. HYBRID: Your own approach balancing these concerns.

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 300-400 words, explain your strategy. Address:
• Which approach will you take and why?
• What are you willing to sacrifice?
• How will you prevent more churn?
• What metrics will you track?

Deadline: 12:00 PM
```

**Narrative Suggestions:**
- Morning briefing: Set the scene, Monday morning alarm
- Email from Customer Success Manager: Detailed churn analysis
- Slack message from Board Member: Expressing concern but supportive

**SESSION 2: Funding Opportunity (Monday PM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Unexpected Angel Investor Interest

At lunch, you receive an email from an angel investor who saw your product on Product Hunt. They're interested in investing $250K on these terms:

OFFER DETAILS:
• Investment: $250,000
• Equity: 15% of company
• Conditions:
  - Must use funds for customer acquisition (marketing/sales)
  - Mandatory monthly board updates
  - Investor gets advisory board seat
  - Acceptance required within 48 hours

YOUR SITUATION:
• Current runway: 8 months (without investment)
• Current burn rate: $62,500/month
• With investment: 12 months runway (but burn increases if you spend it)
• Morning's churn crisis still unresolved

CONSIDERATIONS:
• Taking money means focusing on growth (investor expects it)
• But your product has stability issues (Session 1)
• 15% equity = significant dilution
• Could extend runway by cutting costs instead
• First-time founder - is this a good deal?

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 300-400 words, decide whether to accept the investment. Address:
• Accept, reject, or counter-offer?
• How does this align with Session 1 decisions?
• What's your growth strategy if you accept?
• What's your survival strategy if you reject?
• What questions would you ask the investor?

Deadline: 5:00 PM
```

**Narrative Suggestions:**
- News article: "Angel investors increasingly interested in remote work tools"
- Email from investor: Friendly, enthusiastic tone
- Text from co-founder: "This is amazing! But is it too good to be true?"

**SESSION 3: Engineering Resignation (Tuesday AM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Key Engineer Giving Two Weeks Notice

Your lead engineer (who built 70% of your codebase) just gave two weeks notice. They received an offer from a well-funded competitor:
• $180,000 salary (you're paying $85,000)
• Full benefits
• Larger team (won't be solo anymore)
• Better work-life balance

THIS ENGINEER:
• Knows the entire codebase intimately
• Currently fixing bugs from Session 1 crisis
• Only person who can deploy to production
• Has been working 60-hour weeks
• Expressed burnout 3 months ago (you didn't address it)

YOUR OPTIONS:

A. MATCH OFFER: Pay $180K (burns runway by 4 months)
B. COUNTER: Offer $120K + 2% equity + better hours
C. LET GO: Use two weeks for knowledge transfer, hire replacement
D. NEGOTIATE: More vacation, bonus, flexible work, project of choice
E. OUTSOURCE: Hire offshore team to continue development

CONSTRAINTS:
• Decisions from Sessions 1 & 2 affect available cash
• Raising salaries now sets precedent
• Hiring takes 2-3 months typically
• Your product needs someone who knows the code NOW

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 350-450 words, explain your decision. Address:
• What offer will you make (if any)?
• How will you ensure knowledge transfer?
• Long-term plan for reducing "hero engineer" dependency?
• How does this affect your product roadmap?
• What did you learn about team management?

Deadline: 12:00 PM
```

**SESSION 4: Competitor Launches (Tuesday PM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Major Competitor Announces Free Tier

TechCrunch just published: "FlowMaster Pro Launches Free Forever Plan"

Your main competitor (10x your size) just announced:
• Free tier: Unlimited users, core features
• Paid tier: $29/month (you charge $49)
• They have: 2,000+ customers, $2M funding, strong brand

CUSTOMER REACTION:
• 5 customers emailed asking about your pricing
• Twitter mentions: "Why pay $49 when FlowMaster is free?"
• Your unique features: Better UX, faster support, specific integrations

YOUR CURRENT PRICING:
• $49/month per team
• No free tier
• 17 paying customers (may be fewer after Session 1 churn)
• Converting ~5% of free trials to paid

STRATEGIC OPTIONS:

A. PRICE CUT: Match their $29/month pricing
B. FREEMIUM: Add free tier, risk cannibalizing paid
C. PREMIUM: Raise prices to $79, position as premium
D. VALUE: Double-down on unique features, justify $49
E. PIVOT: Target different customer segment they don't serve

CONSIDERATIONS:
• Your runway is already constrained
• Lower prices = need more volume
• Free tier = support costs without revenue
• Premium position requires exceptional product (you have bugs)

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 350-450 words, explain your pricing strategy. Address:
• What pricing changes (if any) will you make?
• How will you differentiate from free competitor?
• What's your retention plan for existing customers?
• How does this affect your growth projections?
• What's your long-term competitive advantage?

Deadline: 5:00 PM
```

**SESSION 5: Product Direction Fork (Wednesday AM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Two Customer Segments Want Different Things

Your customer base has split into two segments with conflicting needs:

SEGMENT A: Freelancers (10 customers, $49/month = $490 MRR)
• Want: Simple, beautiful, fast
• Complaint: "Too many features I don't need"
• Prefer: Mobile app, basic task management
• Price sensitive

SEGMENT B: Small Agencies (7 customers, $49/month = $343 MRR)
• Want: Advanced features, integrations, reporting
• Complaint: "Missing critical enterprise features"
• Prefer: Desktop, complex workflows
• Willing to pay more ($99-149/month)

FINANCIAL REALITY:
• You can't build for both (small team)
• Freelancers: Larger market, lower revenue per customer
• Agencies: Smaller market, higher revenue per customer
• Your vision was "project management for everyone"

DATA:
• Freelancers: Lower churn (they love simplicity)
• Agencies: Higher churn (missing features)
• Market size: 10M freelancers, 500K agencies
• Competitors: Most target agencies (enterprise)

OPTIONS:

A. FREELANCERS: Simplify product, target consumers
B. AGENCIES: Add features, go upmarket (B2B)
C. SPLIT: Build two separate products
D. COMPROMISE: Try to serve both (risk mediocrity)

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 350-450 words, define your target customer. Address:
• Which segment will you prioritize?
• What features will you build/cut?
• How will you handle the other segment?
• What's your 12-month product vision?
• How does this affect your positioning?

Deadline: 12:00 PM
```

SURVEY RESULTS:

YOUR REFLECTION:

• Team has executed everything you asked
• You haven't checked in on their well-being
• Losing the engineer (Session 3) was a warning sign

CONSTRAINTS:
• Limited budget for raises (affects runway)
• Competitive pressure demands fast action
• Some chaos is normal at startups
• But losing more people would be catastrophic

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 400-500 words, outline your people strategy. Address:
• Immediate actions to improve morale?
• What will you change about your leadership?
• How will you balance growth pressure with team health?
• Compensation: raises, equity, non-monetary perks?
• Communication: how will you create more clarity?

Deadline: 5:00 PM

**SESSION 7: Strategic Partnership Offer (Thursday AM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Enterprise Company Wants to Partner

A Fortune 500 company reached out with a partnership proposal:

THE OFFER:
• They'll integrate your product into their platform
• Distribute to their 50,000 customers
• Co-marketing: Your logo on their site
• White-label version for their enterprise clients

THE CATCH:
• Requires 6 months of engineering work (custom features)
• They pay $0 upfront - revenue share only (30% of sales)
• Exclusive: Can't partner with their competitors
• They control pricing and positioning
• Contract includes performance penalties if you miss deadlines

POTENTIAL UPSIDE:
• Access to 50,000 potential customers
• Credibility (Fortune 500 logo)
• Predictable revenue stream (if successful)
• Validation of your product

POTENTIAL DOWNSIDE:
• 6 months = no work on your core product
• Exclusivity limits other opportunities
• They're a large company - slow, bureaucratic
• If it fails, you've lost 6 months
• Revenue share = less profit per customer

YOUR SITUATION:
• Runway: 5-6 months (depending on earlier decisions)
• Current MRR: ~$800 (varies by churn)
• Team capacity: Already stretched thin
• Need revenue OR need to raise funding soon

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 350-450 words, decide on the partnership. Address:
• Accept, reject, or negotiate different terms?
• How does this fit your strategy from Session 5?
• What are the risks vs. rewards?
• Alternatives if you reject?
• How will you manage this alongside other priorities?

Deadline: 12:00 PM
```

**SESSION 8: Pivot or Persist (Thursday PM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Board Meeting - Discuss Potential Pivot

Your board (if you took investment in Session 2) or advisors want to discuss a pivot:

CURRENT STATE ASSESSMENT:
• MRR Growth: Flat or declining (based on your decisions)
• Churn: Higher than industry standard
• Runway: 3-5 months remaining
• Team: Tired, some may quit
• Product: Works, but not exceptional

PIVOT PROPOSAL:
Based on customer interviews, there's interest in a different product:
• Current: Full project management platform
• Pivot: Simple time-tracking + invoicing tool (smaller scope)
• Market: Less competitive, clearer need
• Build time: 3 months to MVP
• Risks: Start over, abandon current customers

PERSIST CASE:
• You have customers who love your current product
• Pivoting feels like giving up
• All startups face these challenges
• Just need more time and focus
• Problem may be execution, not product

DATA POINTS:
• Successful startups often pivot 2-3 times
• But "pivoting" can also be indecision/fear
• Your current product has shown some traction
• Time is running out (3-5 months runway)

THIRD OPTION:
• "Acqui-hire" offer: Larger company will buy you for $200K
  - Founders get VP roles
  - Team gets jobs
  - No exit payout
  - Product gets shut down

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 400-500 words, make your case. Address:
• Pivot, persist, or accept acquisition?
• What evidence supports your decision?
• If pivoting: What's the new product exactly?
• If persisting: What will you do differently?
• How will you communicate this to your team?

Deadline: 5:00 PM
```

**SESSION 9: Final Sprint (Friday AM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: 90 Days to Success or Shut Down

After Session 8's decisions, you have ~3 months of runway. This is your final strategic plan.

SITUATION:
• Runway: 3 months (90 days)
• Team: Uncertain about future, need clarity
• Product: Whatever you decided in Session 8
• Market: Competitive, fast-moving
• Options: Succeed, raise money, shut down, or get acquired

YOUR GOAL:
Create a detailed 90-day plan to either:
A. Reach profitability (MRR > monthly burn)
B. Hit metrics required to raise funding
C. Achieve acquisition terms you'd accept
D. Gracefully shut down and return remaining cash

KEY QUESTIONS:
• What's your success metric? (MRR target, customer count, etc.)
• What must you accomplish each month?
• What will you stop doing (ruthless prioritization)?
• What resources do you need?
• What could go wrong? (risks and mitigation)

INSPIRATION:
Many successful startups faced "near-death" moments:
• Airbnb: Sold cereal boxes to stay alive
• Notion: Nearly shut down before breakthrough
• Instagram: Pivoted from Burbn to Instagram
• What's your breakthrough going to be?

DECISION REQUIRED:
In 500-600 words, present your 90-day plan. Structure it as:

MONTH 1 (Days 1-30):
• Primary goal: ___
• Key activities: ___
• Success metrics: ___

MONTH 2 (Days 31-60):
• Primary goal: ___
• Key activities: ___
• Success metrics: ___

MONTH 3 (Days 61-90):
• Primary goal: ___
• Key activities: ___
• Success metrics: ___

CONTINGENCY:
• If plan failing at Day 45, what's Plan B?

Deadline: 12:00 PM
```

**SESSION 10: Final Presentation (Friday PM)**

**Challenge:**
```
Title: Present Your Journey & Outcomes

You've completed your 90-day sprint (simulated). Now present your results to stakeholders.

FINAL PRESENTATION INSTRUCTIONS:

Imagine you're presenting to your board, team, and potential investors. Create a reflection covering:

1. JOURNEY SUMMARY (200 words)
• Starting position (Session 1)
• Key decisions made
• Pivots and changes
• Current state

2. STRATEGIC ANALYSIS (300 words)
• What was your overall strategy?
• Which decisions were right/wrong in hindsight?
• What would you do differently?
• What did you learn about trade-offs?

3. FINANCIAL OUTCOMES (150 words)
• MRR trajectory
• Runway management
• Funding decisions
• Break-even status (or not)

4. LEADERSHIP REFLECTION (250 words)
• How did you balance competing priorities?
• Team management successes/failures
• Personal growth as a leader
• Advice for future founders

5. NEXT STEPS (100 words)
• If you succeeded: What's next for the company?
• If you failed: What would you do differently next time?
• Key takeaways

TOTAL: 1,000 words maximum

FORMAT:
• Written report (professional, structured)
• Data/evidence from your decisions
• Honest reflection (not just self-congratulation)

This is your final submission. Make it comprehensive.

Deadline: 5:00 PM
```

## Customization Tips

### Adapting Scenarios to Your Class

**For Different Course Levels:**

**Undergraduate:**
- Simplify financial details
- Add more guidance in prompts
- Focus on fundamental concepts
- Shorter word counts (200-300 words)

**MBA:**
- Use scenarios as written
- Expect sophisticated analysis
- Add financial modeling requirements
- Standard word counts (300-500 words)

**Executive Education:**
- Increase complexity
- Add more ambiguity
- Connect to real current events
- Require implementation plans

### Adjusting Difficulty

**Make Easier:**
- Provide more options (A, B, C choices)
- Include data/financial models
- Offer suggested frameworks
- Shorter challenges (250 words)

**Make Harder:**
- Open-ended scenarios (no options)
- Limited information (ambiguity)
- Interconnected decisions (Session 5 depends on 1-4)
- Longer analyses (500-750 words)

### Industry Customization

**Using Different Industries:**
Each challenge can be adapted:

**Tech SaaS → Manufacturing:**
- "Customer churn" → "Dealer/distributor attrition"
- "Software bugs" → "Quality defects"
- "Feature development" → "Product line expansion"

**E-Commerce → B2B Services:**
- "Inventory" → "Capacity/utilization"
- "Shipping costs" → "Travel expenses"
- "Subscription boxes" → "Service retainers"

### Adding Current Events

**Keep Scenarios Fresh:**
- Reference real companies (with permission)
- Use actual market data
- Incorporate recent trends (AI, remote work, sustainability)
- Update numbers to reflect current economy

Use **Reality Lens** feature to enhance with real-time data!

## Assessment Rubrics

### Sample Scoring Rubric

**Total: 100 points per submission**

| Criterion | Points | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Strategic Thinking** | 40 | Long-term vision, considers multiple perspectives |
| **Analysis** | 25 | Uses data, identifies trade-offs, thorough reasoning |
| **Feasibility** | 20 | Realistic given constraints, actionable |
| **Communication** | 15 | Clear writing, well-structured, professional |

**Scoring Guide:**

**90-100 (Exceptional):**
- Demonstrates sophisticated strategic thinking
- Considers second and third-order effects
- Uses evidence and frameworks effectively
- Writing is clear and compelling

**80-89 (Strong):**
- Solid strategy with clear reasoning
- Good analysis of trade-offs
- Mostly feasible recommendations
- Well-written with minor issues

**70-79 (Good):**
- Competent analysis
- Some strategic thinking
- Generally feasible
- Adequate communication

**60-69 (Adequate):**
- Basic understanding
- Limited analysis
- Some feasibility concerns
- Communication needs improvement

**Below 60 (Needs Improvement):**
- Misunderstands the challenge
- Weak reasoning
- Unrealistic proposals
- Poor communication
