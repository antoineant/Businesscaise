## SLIDE 2: What is BusinessCaise?

```
WHAT IS BUSINESSCAISE?

An interactive business simulation platform where student teams:

📊 Run virtual companies over 10 sessions
💼 Face realistic business challenges
🤝 Make strategic decisions as teams
📈 Compete on leaderboards
🎯 Receive real-time feedback

Duration: Typically 5 days (2 sessions/day)
Format: Team-based (3-4 students per team)
Your Role: Game Master (facilitator, scorer, storyteller)
```

**Speaker Notes:**
- Think of it as a "business RPG" where students are CEOs
- You control the game flow, create scenarios, provide feedback
- Platform handles mechanics (metrics, leaderboards, submissions)
- You bring the pedagogical magic (storytelling, feedback)
- Works for: Strategy, entrepreneurship, management, operations courses

## SLIDE 12: Daily Workflow Overview

```
YOUR DAILY ROUTINE (15-30 min per session)

MORNING:
1. Unlock morning session (1 min)
2. Create/send briefing narrative (5 min)
3. Monitor submissions (ongoing)
4. Score submissions in batches (10-15 min)

AFTERNOON:
5. Unlock afternoon session (1 min)
6. Create contextual narratives (5 min)
7. Continue monitoring & scoring
8. End of day: Review leaderboard

Time commitment: ~2 hours per day (spread throughout)
```

**Speaker Notes:**
- You don't need to babysit it all day
- Check-in pattern: Morning unlock, lunch scoring, afternoon unlock, evening scoring
- Sessions are flexible: Can be shorter (30 min) or longer (90 min) apart
- Most work is in creating narratives and thoughtful feedback
- Platform handles the mechanics

## SLIDE 18: Understanding Metrics

```
HOW METRICS WORK

5 Team Metrics (0-100):
💰 Financial → Revenue, profitability, cash management
⚙️ Operations → Efficiency, quality, execution
📣 Marketing → Brand, positioning, customer acquisition
👥 HR → Employee satisfaction, culture, retention
😊 Customer Satisfaction → Loyalty, NPS, service

Overall Score:
→ Weighted average of all 5 metrics
→ Updates after each scored submission
→ Determines leaderboard position

Your scores (0-100) translate into metric changes:
• 90-100: Significant improvements
• 70-89: Moderate improvements
• 60-69: Slight improvements
• Below 60: Declines
```

**Speaker Notes:**
- Students don't see individual scores (only metrics)
- This focuses them on holistic performance
- Scoring algorithms balance immediate and long-term effects
- Example: Cutting costs (good for Financial, bad for Operations/HR)
- Metrics tell a story of company health over time

## SLIDE 21: Creating Engaging Challenges

```
WHAT MAKES A GOOD CHALLENGE?

✓ Clear: Students understand what's being asked
✓ Realistic: Could happen in real business
✓ Consequential: Choice matters (trade-offs)
✓ Time-bound: Deadline creates urgency
✓ Connected: References earlier decisions

✗ Avoid:
✗ Too theoretical or academic
✗ Obvious "right" answer
✗ Requires external research (unless intended)
✗ Too simple (boring) or too complex (overwhelming)

Structure:
1. Context (what's happening)
2. Situation (the specific problem)
3. Constraints (limitations)
4. Decision Required (what to submit)
```

**Speaker Notes:**
- Use Sample Scenarios as templates
- Best challenges: Multiple valid approaches
- Example: "Cut costs or invest in growth?" → Both can be right
- Build narrative continuity (reference Session 1 in Session 3)
- Adjust difficulty: Start easier (Session 1-2), increase complexity
- Use narratives to add richness and context

## SLIDE 22: Providing Effective Feedback

```
FEEDBACK BEST PRACTICES

Good Feedback:
✓ Specific: "Your cost analysis was thorough"
✓ Actionable: "Next time, consider employee morale"
✓ Balanced: Praise + improvement areas
✓ Timely: Within 24 hours of submission
✓ Consistent: Similar criteria across teams

Templates:

"Strong strategic thinking on [X]. Your analysis of [Y] was particularly insightful. For improvement, consider [Z]. This will help you in future sessions when [context]."

"You identified [X] correctly. However, your plan lacks [Y]. Think about [question] for next time."

Students learn more from feedback than scores!
```

**Speaker Notes:**
- Feedback takes time but has highest pedagogical value
- Avoid generic: "Good job" or "Needs work"
- Specific: Quote their text, reference their reasoning
- Forward-looking: How can they improve in Session 2?
- If a team is struggling: Targeted narrative with hints
- If a team is excelling: Harder follow-up challenges

## SLIDE 23: Managing Pace & Difficulty

```
PACING THE SIMULATION

Day 1: ESTABLISH RHYTHM
→ Simpler challenges
→ Get everyone comfortable
→ Check for technical issues

Day 2-3: BUILD COMPLEXITY
→ Increase ambiguity
→ Introduce competing pressures
→ Raise stakes

Day 4: PEAK CHALLENGE
→ Most difficult scenarios
→ Multiple simultaneous issues
→ Test strategic thinking

Day 5: INTEGRATION & REFLECTION
→ Bring threads together
→ Final strategic decision
→ Reflection submission

Adjust based on student feedback!
```

**Speaker Notes:**
- You control the difficulty curve
- If students struggling: Slow down, provide more context
- If students breezing through: Add complexity, remove guidance
- Check mid-week: Are submissions improving?
- Balance: Challenge them but don't overwhelm
- Day 5: Consider culminating presentation instead of challenge

## SLIDE 24: Common Pitfalls to Avoid

```
MISTAKES TO AVOID

❌ Too Much, Too Fast
→ Don't unlock multiple sessions at once
→ Students need time to discuss and decide

❌ Inconsistent Scoring
→ Use rubric, score same session together
→ Take breaks between scoring sessions

❌ Forgetting Narratives
→ Narratives bring it alive!
→ Minimum: 1 briefing per day

❌ No Mid-Week Check-In
→ Talk to class on Day 3
→ Address confusion, celebrate highlights

❌ Perfectionism
→ First time won't be perfect - that's OK!
→ Iterate and improve next semester

❌ Neglecting Struggling Teams
→ Monitor all teams, provide support
→ Learning > competition
```

**Speaker Notes:**
- Learn from pilot experiences
- Biggest mistake: Unlocking too fast (students feel rushed)
- Second biggest: Not providing enough feedback
- Remember: You're facilitating learning, not running Olympics
- It's okay to adjust mid-stream (extend deadline, etc.)
- Document what works for next time

- Keep pace brisk (don't drag)
- Use variety: Lecture → Demo → Hands-on
- Break at 45 minutes if 90-minute session
- Celebrate small wins ("Great, you unlocked a session!")

**Common Questions & Answers:**

**Q: "I'm not technical. Can I do this?"**
A: "Yes! It's designed for faculty, not developers. If you can use email and Google Docs, you can use this."

**Q: "What if students cheat?"**
A: "The nature of the questions makes 'cheating' hard - there are no right answers. Focus grading on reasoning, not outcomes."

**Q: "Can I reuse content from semester to semester?"**
A: "Yes! Save your best narratives and challenges. Build a library over time."

**Q: "What's the student feedback been?"**
A: "In pilots, 85%+ of students rate it as 'most valuable' course component. High engagement."

**Q: "Does this work for online/hybrid classes?"**
A: "Yes! Platform is fully online. Works great for remote learning. Teams can collaborate via Zoom/Slack."
